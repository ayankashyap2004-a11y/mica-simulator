# MICA Simulator: RPG design research

*Planning only, 2 Nov 2026. Nothing here is implemented yet.*

**The brief:**
- **What it is:** a third-person open-world campus game set at MICA, Ahmedabad. Its structure follows Bully (an open-world school) and its storytelling follows Life is Strange (choices with consequences).
- **Who writes it:** Ayan writes the NPCs and storylines. This document covers what the best games in the genre do, why it works, and what that means for us.
- **Audience:** MICAns and applicants first. A portfolio piece later.

---

## 1. Reference games and why they work

| Game | Why it is engaging | Take for MICA Sim | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bully** (Rockstar, 2006) | Rockstar's open world shrunk to one school. Cliques give the world a social map. Classes are minigames that unlock abilities: Chemistry gives firecrackers, English helps you talk out of trouble, Gym gives moves. A timetable, curfew and prefects make rule-breaking fun. The map opens up over a full school year that changes with the seasons. | A timetable you can skip at a cost. Classes as short minigames that reward real abilities. Committees and groups as cliques. Campus areas and events unlock across the term. | Cartoon stereotypes. Critics found some of Bully's characters cliché. |
| **Life is Strange** (Dontnod, 2015) and **True Colors** (Deck Nine, 2021) | Choices with short-, mid- and long-term consequences. Build empathy for a person before asking the moral question. Make the two options in a split polar opposites. Give every NPC a name and one beat of their own. Optional "sit and reflect" moments. Regional slang ("hella") makes the world feel real. Script scenes like a screenplay around one moment the whole team is excited about. | Choices that land on people, with layered consequences and journal entries. MICA slang. Optional sit-down moments at Chhota, the OAT or the hostel roof. One standout moment per episode. | Over-signalling which choices matter. An ending that ignores your earlier choices. Too many voices in the player's head. Cartoony voice acting. |
| **Persona 5 Royal** (Atlus) | A calendar where every free afternoon and evening is a trade-off between classes, a job, friends and the main plot. Confidants are tied to particular days and stats. Scarce time makes each choice mean something. | Free time slots that you spend on people (bonds) or on goals. Some people are only around on some days. | Pressure that makes players anxious, and content that is easy to miss without a guide. |
| **Fire Emblem: Three Houses** | School life at a monastery: weekdays for teaching, free days for exploring, a story mission each month. Birthdays and holidays are marked on the calendar. A bulletin board offers quests you can finish the same day. | A term calendar with fests and deadlines. A notice board for small same-day tasks. | Calendar icons that are never explained. |
| **Red Dead Redemption 2** | Named NPCs with daily routines who react to you again on a second visit. Stranger missions are varied and surprising. The world carries on without you. | Routines for key NPCs (mess, KEIC, Chhota, hostel). NPCs who remember your last choice. | Large-scale simulation that a small team cannot build. |
| **The Witcher 3** | Side quests written with the same care as the main story. Simple "kill the monster" quests turn out to be stories about people. Notice boards and NPCs make the world feel independent of you. | Every side quest about a person, never "fetch 5 items". | Repetitive "follow the trail" quest steps. |
| **Yakuza / Like a Dragon** | A small, dense map packed with absurd, funny side stories next to a serious main plot. The director says a strong main dish alone won't earn a Michelin star. The side stories are found on the street, not on a checklist. | Density over size; MICA is about 19 acres. Funny side stories hidden around campus. | Humour that tips from charming into cringe. |
| **Night in the Woods** | A daily routine of waking up, checking your phone and walking the same streets. Small-town friends who feel real. Minor characters change over time. You earn the backstory instead of being told it. Dark themes under a cute art style. | Hostel life as the routine. A small core friend group. Side characters whose lives move on through the term. | Slow pacing and repetition. |
| **Stardew Valley** | Villagers have default routines that change with weather, weekday, festivals and friendship level. Heart events unlock with closeness. Seasonal festivals give the year a rhythm and something to look forward to. | NPC routines that depend on the day, plus friendship tiers that unlock personal scenes. Fests (MICANVAS, Oorja) as calendar anchors. | Too much daily grind. |
| **Disco Elysium** | The dialogue is the gameplay. Skills speak as voices in your head, and "thoughts" you take on change your options. | The skills a MICAn grows (pitching, research, design, networking) open new dialogue options. Winning an argument or a pitch instead of a fight. | Walls of text. |
| **Venba** | Cooking is both the puzzle and the story. Very specific Tamil culture made players everywhere think of their own families. | Specific beats only MICA has: chai at Chhota, the Chhota art wall, hostel graffiti, case comps. Being specific is what makes it feel universal. | Dialogue choices that make no difference. |
| **Hogwarts Legacy** (cautionary) | A famous school used only as a backdrop: no timetable, no curfew, few relationships. Critics repeatedly said Bully did it better with less. | Proof that the school structure is the game. | Treating classes and relationships as decoration. |

### What the hits have in common
Each driver below maps to self-determination theory (Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski, 2006). That research found that autonomy, competence and relatedness each predict how much players enjoy a game and whether they keep playing.

| Driver | Meaning | Need it meets |
|---|---|---|
| **Scarce time** | You can't do everything today, so what you choose says who you are. | Autonomy |
| **People with inner lives** | Named NPCs with their own beat, routine and change over time. | Relatedness |
| **Consequences that return** | Short-, mid- and long-term effects, shown on people rather than as a score. | Autonomy |
| **School rules to push against** | A timetable, curfew and authority give rule-breaking its fun. | Autonomy and competence |
| **Density and surprise** | Small map, many hand-made stories, found on foot. | Competence and curiosity |
| **Specific culture** | Real slang, places and rituals. "Know what you write." | Presence and relatedness |
| **Feel-it moments** | Planned emotional scenes with music, and quiet optional moments. | Presence |

## 2. Points of Parity: what players will expect
These are the basics. Being without them would feel broken, though they don't make the game stand out.

1. **Free roaming** across a recognisable world. We already have this.
2. **A main storyline plus side quests**, with a quest log and markers on the map or minimap.
3. **Named NPCs** with portraits, simple routines and remembered relationships.
4. **Branching dialogue** with visible choices and consequences you can track (a journal or "people remember this").
5. **Cutscenes** for key beats, plus light in-world dialogue for the small ones.
6. **Time passing:** day and night, plus calendar days or a term (see section 4).
7. **Progression:** skills, items and unlocks earned through classes, quests and bonds.
8. **Minigames and activities:** classes, sports, Chhota hangouts, campus fests.
9. **Saving and continuing:** autosave and multiple slots. A browser game needs local saves.
10. **Accessibility:** skip or replay cutscenes, subtitles, adjustable text speed, and no punishing timers by default.

## 3. Points of Difference: why MICA Sim, specifically
These are the reasons someone would play this over anything else.

1. **A real, accurately traced campus.** It is built from satellite imagery and the architect's building schedule, and checked on the ground by a student. No other game recreates MICA, and we found no notable game set at any Indian university.
2. **Insider culture as content:**
   - **Places:** Chhota as the 24-hour heart of campus, the OAT, the hostel courtyards, Shishya Gramah.
   - **Events:** MICANVAS, Oorja garba, committees, case competitions, late nights at KEIC, the placement season.
   - **Language:** MICA slang. Applicants get to see the real place; alumni get nostalgia.
3. **The School of Ideas as a mechanic.** You win with ideas, not fists. Pitches, campaign briefs, design critiques and debates are the "combat", in the way Bully used English class to talk your way out of trouble and Disco Elysium makes dialogue the fight.
4. **Written by a MICAn.** Authentic by default, in the same way Life is Strange's writers grounded it in research and a diverse writers' room.
5. **Browser-native.** One link, no install, runs on a student laptop. That suits sharing in batch WhatsApp groups.
6. **An Indian setting with Indian texture:** chai, a security uncle at the gate, Ahmedabad heat and monsoon, garba nights, hostel rules. This is what Venba showed works: the specific feels universal.
7. **Real time anchors.** The in-game term can follow MICA's academic calendar: induction, terms, MICANVAS, placements and convocation.

## 4. Time system: calendar of days or real-time day/night

| | **Calendar of days** (Persona, Fire Emblem) | **Real-time day/night** (RDR2, Stardew within a day) |
|---|---|---|
| How it works | The day splits into slots (morning class, afternoon, evening, night). Choosing an activity uses up a slot. | A continuous clock. NPCs move on schedules, and things happen at set times. |
| Strengths | Every choice is weighty and readable. Easy to write for, since scenes happen at set times. Fits "classes vs. free time" naturally. Cheaper to build. | Feels alive and open-world. Suits free roaming, which is our engine's strength. Natural for Bully-style curfew and "skip class to sneak around". |
| Weaknesses | Less of an open-world feel. Time stops while you roam. Risk of a menu-driven game. | Hard to guarantee the player is there for scripted scenes. Easy to miss content. Hard to tune pacing. NPC routines cost more. |
| Risks | Anxiety and content you can miss without a guide (a common Persona complaint). | Players getting lost and nothing happening. "I missed the event because I was walking." |
| Writing effort | Low to medium. | Medium to high. |
| Fit with our build | Fair. | Strong. |

**Recommendation: the Bully hybrid.** Bully itself does this.
- **Within a day:** a real-time clock runs while you roam, compressed so one in-game day takes about 20–30 minutes. Classes have fixed times, and there's a curfew.
- **Across days:** a term calendar with fixed dated beats (fests, deadlines, placements). Main-story scenes are dated, and side quests wait for you.
- **Missing things:** only a few choices can be missed, and the game always points them out ("Tonight: Oorja at the OAT"). Bonds never lock forever.

## 5. Proposed design pillars
1. **Real campus, alive.** Accurate places that change with the term: fest decorations, exam-week quiet, monsoon.
2. **Days have weight.** Limited free time. Who you spend it with defines your MICA.
3. **Choices land on people.** Build empathy before the decision. Consequences come back days later through people, not as a score.
4. **Small and dense.** Every corner of campus has a hand-made story. No filler fetch quests.
5. **Ideas win.** Pitches, briefs and debates are the challenges, and MICA skills open new dialogue.

## 6. Templates for writing NPCs and storylines (for Ayan)

**NPC card:**
- **Identity:** name, programme and batch, hostel.
- **Grounding:** a MICA archetype, kept fictional, never a real person.
- **Routine:** where they are, morning, afternoon and night.
- **Inner life:** want versus need, a secret, how they talk (slang, catchphrase).
- **Their "one beat":** the small moment that defines them.
- **Bond:** levels 1–5 and what unlocks at each.
- **Memory:** which of your choices they remember.

**Quest card:**
- **Hook:** who asks, and where.
- **Steps:** at most 3–5.
- **The choice:** two options that are polar opposites, both understandable.
- **Consequences:** right away / a few days later / end of term.
- **Reward:** skill, item or bond.
- **Its place in the calendar:** dated, or open whenever.

**Episode or chapter:**
- **One big moment** the whole chapter is built around.
- **Optional quiet moments.**
- **A cliffhanger.**
- **Cutscenes:** at most 2–3 per episode, each under 90 seconds and skippable.

---

## 7. Pitfalls to avoid
- **Real people:** never use real students or faculty by name or likeness. Use fictional composites, and ask permission even for cameos.
- **School as backdrop:** the Hogwarts Legacy problem. Classes and relationships must matter.
- **Illusion of choice:** don't over-signal which choices count, and don't make the ending ignore them.
- **Scope creep:** a small team can't simulate RDR2. Hand-place routines for 15–25 key NPCs and keep the rest ambient.
- **Cringe humour:** be funny about real MICA life, not stereotypes.
- **Missed content:** signpost dated events, and let bonds be recovered.
- **Over-long cutscenes:** short, skippable and replayable.

## 8. Open questions for Ayan
1. **Protagonist:** fixed (named, with a personality, like Max or Chloe) or customisable? Life is Strange found a defined lead builds more empathy.
2. **Timeline:** one term, or the full two-year PGP with placements as the finale?
3. **Tone:** grounded and heartfelt (Night in the Woods), or more comic chaos (Bully, Yakuza)?
4. **Supernatural twist:** any magical-realism element (Life is Strange's rewind), or purely real life?
5. **Main story theme:** belonging, ambition, friendship, mental health, placements?
